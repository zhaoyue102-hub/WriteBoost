        Common Words to Upgrade

        Writing Skills

        Show, Don't Tell

        Instead of saying "She was scared," show it: "Her hands trembled as she backed toward the door."

        He was angry.
        His face turned red, and he slammed his fist on the table.
        Build Tension

        Use short sentences and sensory details to create suspense and keep readers engaged.

        Then the door opened.
        The door handle turned. Slowly. She held her breath.
        Dialogue

        Use quotes for speaking characters. Add action beats to show who's speaking.

        He said he was leaving and went.
        "I'm leaving," he said, grabbing his coat from the chair.
        Strong Verbs

        Replace weak verbs with powerful ones. "Walked" becomes "stomped," "crept," or "marched."

        She walked into the room.
        She stormed into the room, her eyes blazing.
        Transition Words

        Use words like "however," "meanwhile," "suddenly" to connect ideas smoothly.

        The sun set. The stars appeared.
        As the sun set, the stars began to appear one by one.
        Sensory Details

        Engage all 5 senses: sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch to bring scenes to life.

        The kitchen smelled.
        The kitchen smelled of fresh bread and brewing coffee.

        Show, Don't Tell Drills

        Rewrite each sentence to show instead of tell. Use senses, actions, and body language instead of directly naming emotions!

        Boring sentence:
        He was nervous.
        Show his body. Does he fidget? Sweat? What does his voice do?

        Quick Start
        What this app is for WriteBoost trains narrative skills (story, voice, sensory detail, structure)⁠. Don’t expect modules for thesis papers, citations, or persuasive five-paragraph essays — the prompts and feedback assume you’re writing fiction or story-like scenes⁠.
        1) Start on Prompt Pick one of six prompts (Refresh for new choices); it loads into Write⁠. You can also open Write directly from the tab bar⁠.
        2) Write Optional word goal and a timer (under Story structure on the right on wide screens)⁠. Aim for at least 50 story words, then Submit⁠. Challenge cards don’t count toward that minimum⁠.
        3) Get unstuck Use Challenge in the editor, open the Power Words modal, or visit Learn → Power Words.
        4) Results Score breakdown, coaching, outline, optional online grammar check. Revise this draft returns you to Write⁠; submit again to see the revision comparison on Results⁠.
        5) Progress and Data Skill charts and recent-essay trends are at the top; essay history, export / import, and clear-all are below on the same page. Use the header Passcode control to switch writers or create a new passcode. Tap header Avg or Best for skill breakdown; Essays / Words for the library. Open Achievements for badges⁠.
        6) Shortcuts Ctrl+Enter / ⌘+Enter submits from Write. Esc closes dialogs⁠.
        Self-Review Before Submitting

        Optional pause before scores: use the checklist for your own reflection. If you enter My estimated score (0–100), that guess is saved for the Self Reviewer badge — otherwise submitting works the same.
